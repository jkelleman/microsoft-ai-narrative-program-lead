# Executive One-Pager Template: AI Program Status & Ask

**Author:** Jennifer Kelleman
**Purpose:** Leave-behind artifact for every executive briefing — one page, distributed after the meeting, within 24 hours
**Format:** Print-ready at 8.5×11 or readable as a PDF attachment. Replace all bracketed values with actuals before distributing.

---

## How to Use This Template

This one-pager is not a slide. It is a document — designed to be read, forwarded, and referenced between briefings.

Every executive briefing produces one. Every one-pager follows the same structure so that executives can find the information they need without re-learning the format. Consistency builds trust faster than novelty.

**Rule:** One page. No exceptions. If it doesn't fit, cut it — don't shrink the font.

## One-Pager Design Principles

These principles apply regardless of which template is in use:

**1. One metric — not five.**
Choosing one metric forces the program lead to have a clear point of view on what matters most right now. Executives remember one number. They don't remember dashboards.

**2. The insight is new information.**
The status update tells the executive what happened. The insight tells them something they didn't know. A one-pager without a genuine insight is a report. A one-pager with an insight is a briefing.

**3. The ask is specific and time-bounded.**
"We'd love your support" is not an ask. "We need your approval for [X] before [DATE] so that [CONSEQUENCE] doesn't happen" is an ask. Every one-pager ends with one — never zero, never two.

**4. The next steps are milestones, not tasks.**
Milestones are business-visible checkpoints. Tasks are engineering activity. Executives should be able to read the "What's Next" section and understand what they will be able to see at each point — not what the team will be doing.

**5. One page, always.**
If you can't summarize the program state on one page, you don't have a clear enough picture of the program state. The constraint is a discipline, not a limitation.
