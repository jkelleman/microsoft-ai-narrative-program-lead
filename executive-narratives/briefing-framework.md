# Executive Briefing Framework: Enterprise AI Programs

**Author:** Jennifer Kelleman
**Purpose:** Repeatable framework for designing and delivering C-suite AI briefings

---

## Why Briefing Frameworks Matter

The most common failure mode in enterprise AI programs isn't the technology — it's the executive narrative.

Programs stall when:
- Leaders lose sight of progress because milestones aren't communicated in terms they care about
- Business outcomes are buried under technical detail
- Briefings are informational but not actionable
- Every briefing feels like the first one — no arc, no momentum

A briefing framework solves this. It ensures every executive engagement, regardless of who is presenting or which phase the program is in, tells a coherent, cumulative story that builds confidence and maintains the mandate.

---

## Framework Architecture

Every briefing in this framework is built on four structural elements:

### Element 1: The Anchor (2 minutes)
**Purpose:** Remind the audience why they're in the room.

The Anchor reconnects the business problem to the briefing. It starts with *their* stated priority — not the program's priority. This is the difference between an executive who leans in and one who checks their phone.

> *"The last time we met, you told me that improving customer churn prediction was a top-three priority for the year. Today I'm going to show you where we are on the data foundation that makes that possible — and what's changed since our last conversation."*

Key: The anchor should feel like you've been listening, not presenting.

### Element 2: The Situation (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Show the current state clearly and honestly.

The Situation uses data and evidence — not narrative — to establish the current state of the program. This is where the AI Readiness Scorecard, quality metrics, and milestone tracking live.

Structure:
- What was the goal for this period?
- What happened?
- What does the data show?

The Situation should be visually supported. A single Power BI dashboard pinned to the briefing is more effective than 20 slides.

### Element 3: The Insight (5–10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Tell them something they didn't know before this meeting.

This is the highest-value portion of any briefing. It answers: *"What has this program taught us about our business?"*

Examples:
- "We discovered that 23% of your transaction records have inconsistent customer IDs across systems — which means your current churn model is working with fragmented signals."
- "The Customer domain data quality score jumped from 61% to 84% this quarter. That's the threshold we needed to reach before grounding the first AI application."
- "Your Product catalog is the lowest-scored domain for AI readiness. Here's why, and here's what we're doing about it."

Insights should be *specific, evidence-based, and connected to business implications*. Generic observations are a missed opportunity.

### Element 4: The Ask (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** End every briefing with a clear, single ask.

Executive briefings that end with "any questions?" leave energy on the table. Every briefing should end with one of three ask types:

| Ask Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Decision** | "We need a decision on whether to extend the program into the Operations domain before next month's capacity planning cycle." |
| **Endorsement** | "We'd like you to share the AI readiness results with the board as evidence of progress on the data strategy." |
| **Resource** | "To hit the Phase 3 timeline, we need two additional data engineers assigned by the end of this month." |

The ask should be specific, time-bounded, and require a response — not just acknowledgment.

## The Cumulative Arc

Individual briefings are strong. A *series* of briefings with a coherent arc is transformational.

Each briefing in the arc should:
1. Reference what was committed to in the last briefing (and whether it happened)
2. Present the evidence of progress (the Situation)
3. Deepen the insight (they should know more after each briefing)
4. Move the ask forward (each ask should build on the last)

### Four-Briefing Arc for AI Data Modernization

| Briefing | Theme | Key Message | Ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | The Opportunity | "Here's the AI opportunity and why data is the dependency." | Approve the program charter and resource plan |
| **2** | The Discovery | "Here's what we found in your data estate." | Prioritize remediation of the top 3 critical findings |
| **3** | The Foundation | "Here's the governed estate we've built. Here's the proof." | Endorse the AI readiness scorecard as the go/no-go gate |
| **4** | The Launch | "You're ready for AI in these domains. Here's the roadmap." | Approve Phase 4: first production AI application |

Each briefing should feel like the *natural next chapter* of a story that started at Briefing 1.

## Audience Archetypes and Adaptation

Different executives need different entry points to the same story.

### The CIO
**What they care about:** Platform risk, vendor consolidation, team capability, total cost of ownership.

**How to adapt the framework:**
- Lead the Situation with architecture decisions and platform metrics (CU utilization, workspace consolidation, licensing efficiency)
- The Insight should connect to platform risk reduction: "We've eliminated X copies of production data, reducing our attack surface and licensing exposure."
- The Ask should be a platform decision (capacity, governance policy, enterprise agreement)

### The CDO / VP Data
**What they care about:** Data trust, governance maturity, team credibility, cross-functional adoption.

**How to adapt the framework:**
- Lead the Situation with governance metrics (sensitivity label coverage, ownership documentation, lineage completeness)
- The Insight should surface a data quality finding with business implications
- The Ask should be a governance decision (access policy, data ownership escalation, business glossary priority)

### The CEO / Business Unit Leader
**What they care about:** Revenue impact, customer experience, competitive differentiation, time to value.

**How to adapt the framework:**
- Skip the technical foundation. Lead directly with: "This program is what makes [AI capability] possible. Here's where we are."
- The Insight should be expressed as a business metric change, not a data metric change
- The Ask should be business-level: a champion, a use case prioritization, a budget commitment

## Briefing Anti-Patterns

These are the patterns that undermine executive briefings, even when the underlying work is excellent:

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **The Demo Trap** | Technology demos impress engineers, not executives. They create "so what?" reactions. | Lead with the business outcome the demo enables, not the demo itself |
| **The Progress Report** | "We completed 12 of 15 milestones" is informational, not compelling. | Frame progress as *trust earned* and *risk reduced*, not tasks completed |
| **The Hedging Narrative** | "We think this might eventually..." signals low conviction. | State what you know clearly; acknowledge unknowns without apologizing for them |
| **The Detail Dump** | Briefings loaded with technical detail shift cognitive load to the audience. | One dashboard, three insights, one ask. Everything else is backup. |
| **The Missing Ask** | Briefings that end without an ask leave executives feeling like spectators. | Always end with a specific, time-bounded ask that requires a response |

---

## Briefing Preparation Checklist

Before every executive briefing, confirm:

- [ ] The Anchor references the executive's stated priority, not the program's priority
- [ ] The Situation is supported by current data (run the AI readiness scorecard before the briefing)
- [ ] The Insight is specific, evidence-based, and new information the executive doesn't already have
- [ ] The Ask is a single request that is specific and time-bounded
- [ ] Backup materials are prepared but not planned for presentation
- [ ] The cumulative arc is maintained: this briefing references the last one and sets up the next one
- [ ] An executive summary (1 page max) is available for post-briefing distribution
