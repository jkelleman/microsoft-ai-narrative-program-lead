# CEO & Board Briefing Template: Agentic AI — From Data Estate to Autonomous Intelligence

**Template Version:** 1.0
**Author:** Jennifer Kelleman
**Use:** Customize for CEO, board of directors, or executive committee engagements. Replace `[COMPANY]`, `[DATE]`, and all bracketed values with specifics. Remove speaker notes before distributing.

---

## Pre-Briefing Setup

**Duration:** 20 minutes (15 min briefing + 5 min discussion / Ask)
**Format:** In-person or video; single visual — one page, no slides unless board format requires them
**Attendees:** CEO, Board member(s), or Executive Committee; CFO if investment decision is on the table
**Materials to bring:**
- This briefing doc (speaker notes only)
- One-page executive summary (see `/executive-narratives/executive-one-pager-template.md`)
- AI Readiness Scorecard dashboard link — open before the meeting, do not present unless asked

**Tone calibration:**
This is a business conversation, not a technology briefing. The word "Fabric" does not appear. The phrase "agentic AI" is defined in plain language the first time it's used. Every metric connects to a dollar, a customer, or a competitive position.

---

## The Anchor (2 minutes)

> "[COMPANY] made a strategic decision [N] months ago to invest in the data foundation that would make AI real — not as a proof of concept, but as a production capability.
>
> That investment is paying off. And the reason I'm here today is that we are now standing at the next decision point — one that will determine whether [COMPANY] leads in the emerging category of *agentic AI*, or watches competitors do it first.
>
> I want to give you a clear picture of where we are, what's now possible, and what we need from leadership to move."

**Speaker notes:**
- If this is a board setting, open with the strategic framing before the anchor: "The data strategy investment approved [DATE] was a prerequisite for what I'm about to show you."
- Do not open with a technology definition. Open with the business outcome that's at stake.
- The phrase "agentic AI" should feel like a natural extension of AI, not a new category requiring a long explanation.

---

## The Situation (5 minutes)

> "Here is where we are in plain terms.
>
> Eighteen months ago, [COMPANY]'s data was fragmented across [N] systems with no common governance model and no clear picture of which data was trustworthy enough to act on. AI investments were failing in production — not because the AI was wrong, but because the data it was grounded in wasn't reliable.
>
> Today that's changed. We have a governed, unified data estate. [N] domains are AI-ready — meaning we know the data is complete, consistent, and trustworthy enough to put AI on top of it. That work was done by our data engineering organization, and it's the infrastructure behind everything I'm about to describe.
>
> The current state, in three numbers:
> - **[N] of [TOTAL] priority data domains** are AI-ready today
> - **[X]% reduction in data preparation time** for analysts — which translates directly to faster decision cycles
> - **[Y] weeks** to stand up a new AI application from a domain that has already been modernized, down from [BEFORE] months
>
> That's the foundation. What I want to talk about now is what we build on top of it."

**Speaker notes:**
- Keep the numbers to three. CEOs and board members lose the thread after three.
- "Data engineering organization" is the right attribution — it names the team doing the work and positions it as a strategic function, not a support function.
- If a board member asks a technical question here, acknowledge it and offer to follow up: "That's a good question for the technical deep-dive — I want to stay on the business picture for now and I'll make sure you get that answer."

## The Insight (6 minutes)

*Lead with the definition, then the business implication. Choose the insight option that matches this audience's priority.*

### Insight Option A: The Competitive Inflection Point

> "The shift that's happening in enterprise AI right now is from AI that *assists* to AI that *acts*.
>
> The first wave of enterprise AI was about helping people work faster — a Copilot that summarizes documents, a model that flags anomalies in a report. Those applications are valuable and [COMPANY] has several of them running today.
>
> The second wave is different. Agentic AI — AI that can take a goal, break it into tasks, execute those tasks autonomously, and report back on the outcome — is moving from research to production deployments at companies like [COMPETITOR EXAMPLE if appropriate].
>
> The critical difference between companies that will lead in this category and companies that will follow is not the AI. The AI is largely the same. It's the data estate underneath it. Agentic AI requires data it can trust implicitly — because there's no human in the loop reviewing every step.
>
> [COMPANY] has that data estate. Most of your competitors do not yet. The window where that's a competitive advantage is not permanent."

### Insight Option B: The Operational Value Unlock

> "I want to show you what agentic AI looks like in your business specifically.
>
> Right now, your data engineering team spends approximately [X]% of their capacity on pipeline monitoring — manually checking whether data is fresh, complete, and meeting quality standards before it reaches the analysts and AI applications that depend on it.
>
> With an agentic AI layer on top of the data estate we've built, that monitoring becomes autonomous. An AI agent watches the pipeline, detects quality degradation before it reaches production, diagnoses the root cause, and in low-risk scenarios, self-heals. In higher-risk scenarios, it escalates with a diagnosis and a recommended fix already prepared.
>
> The result: your data engineering team spends [Y]% of their capacity on pipeline maintenance instead of [X]%. The difference goes into building the next AI-ready domain — compounding the competitive advantage instead of maintaining the existing one.
>
> The infrastructure to do this is already built. The question is whether we move now."

### Insight Option C: The Risk Frame

> "I want to be direct about the risk that's accumulating on the other side of this decision.
>
> Enterprise AI programs are entering a new phase. The companies that are three to five years into serious data modernization work are now deploying autonomous agents — in customer service, in supply chain, in financial operations. Those deployments are producing compounding advantages: better data, better models, better agents.
>
> Companies that haven't done the data foundation work can't catch up quickly. The foundation takes 18 to 24 months. There's no shortcut. The companies that are behind on data modernization are going to be behind on agentic AI by the same margin.
>
> [COMPANY] is ahead of most. But that advantage requires continued investment to maintain. What I'm asking for today is the investment that keeps us on the right side of that gap."

## The Ask (3 minutes)

*Select one ask. Never bring more than one to a CEO or board briefing.*

### Ask Type: Strategic Investment Decision

> "Here's what I'm asking for today.
>
> To move from our AI-ready data estate to a production agentic AI capability, we need [SPECIFIC INVESTMENT — e.g., 'approval to expand the data engineering program into a 6-month Agentic AI phase, with a budget of $[X]'].
>
> The business case is straightforward: this investment will [OUTCOME, e.g., 'reduce pipeline maintenance overhead by 40%, freeing capacity for three new AI-ready domain builds'] and position [COMPANY] to launch [SPECIFIC AI APPLICATION] in production by [DATE].
>
> The alternative is waiting. And waiting means our competitors' compounding data advantage continues to grow while ours stays flat.
>
> Can I get approval to move forward today?"

### Ask Type: Champion Endorsement

> "I'm not asking for budget today. I'm asking for something more valuable: your sponsorship.
>
> The agentic AI program I'm proposing is technically ready to launch. The data estate supports it. The engineering team has a clear plan.
>
> What will determine whether it succeeds is executive visibility. When the business units hear that [CEO NAME] or the board sees this as a strategic priority, adoption accelerates. Blockers that take weeks to resolve get resolved in days.
>
> I'm asking you to publicly endorse this as a [COMPANY] strategic initiative at [UPCOMING FORUM — e.g., 'the Q3 all-hands']. That endorsement is worth more than any budget line."

### Ask Type: Board Report Inclusion

> "I want to ask you to include our AI Readiness Scorecard in the next board report — as a standing metric alongside revenue, cost, and customer satisfaction.
>
> Here's why this matters beyond optics: boards that track AI readiness as a metric create accountability for it across the leadership team. It stops being 'the data team's project' and becomes an organizational capability that everyone owns.
>
> I can have a board-ready one-page version prepared by [DATE]. Would you be willing to include it?"

## Closing the Briefing

After the Ask, stop speaking. Wait for a response.

If the response is affirmative, confirm the specific commitment:

> "Thank you. I'll follow up with [specific next step] by [specific date]. I'll also make sure [CDO / CIO / program lead] has visibility into what was committed here so the program can move immediately."

If the response is a request for more information:

> "Understood. What would be most useful — a technical deep-dive with the engineering team, a financial model with the CFO, or a competitive analysis? I want to make sure you have exactly what you need to make this decision, not everything I could possibly give you."

If the response is a deferral:

> "I hear that. What's the decision-making timeline, and is there anything I can do to accelerate it? I want to be respectful of your process and also honest that the competitive window I described isn't indefinite."

## Adaptation Notes for Different Board Contexts

| Context | Emphasis | What to remove |
|---|---|---|
| **Audit / Risk Committee** | Data governance maturity, AI risk controls, agent guardrails | Competitive framing |
| **Compensation / HR Committee** | Workforce impact, data engineering talent strategy | Platform technical detail |
| **Investment / Finance Committee** | ROI model, cost of delay, build vs. buy | Narrative/story elements |
| **Full Board — strategic update** | Competitive position, investment thesis, capability timeline | All technical detail |
